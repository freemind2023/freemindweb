Competitors Fund Your Research: Using Meta Ads Library

By Aryan Ahire, Founder — Free Mind Consultancy · Published September 24, 2026 · Part of 100 Days of Marketing Engineering

Your competitor is paying Meta to do your market research for you. Every ad they run, they've already tested with real money and real audiences. Meta's Ad Library makes those ads public, so you can read their hooks, offers and creatives without spending anything.

What Meta Ads Library is

The Meta Ad Library is a free, searchable public database of ads running across Meta's platforms, including Facebook and Instagram. You can search by advertiser name or keyword and see the ads a page is currently running. It's public by design, for transparency, so using it for research is exactly what it's there for.

What you can learn from one competitor's ads

  • Hooks — the first line or first seconds designed to stop the scroll.
  • Offers — free consultation, discount, free trial, lead magnet, limited-time deal.
  • Creative format — static image, video, carousel, testimonial, founder talking to camera.
  • Messaging angles — pain point, result, social proof, fear of missing out, price.
  • Calls to action — book a call, WhatsApp, sign up, learn more.
  • Testing — several variations of one idea running at once usually means they're testing it.

The workflow

List→ Search→ Record→ Find patterns→ Decide
  • List 10 to 20 competitors, direct and adjacent.
  • Search each one in the Ad Library and open their active ads.
  • Record each ad in a spreadsheet: advertiser, hook, offer, format, angle, call to action, and when it started running.
  • Find patterns across the whole sheet, not inside one ad.
  • Decide which angles and offers to test in your own campaigns.

Look for the pattern, not the single ad

One ad tells you almost nothing. Twenty competitors' ads tell you a lot. Ask:

  • What are they repeatedly advertising? The same service or product across many ads is their priority.
  • Which offers keep coming back? An offer that appears again and again is probably working well enough to keep funding.
  • What messages are they testing? Several versions of a hook or creative for one offer is an active test.
  • What is nobody saying? An angle none of them uses may be an opening for you.

Build the competitor intelligence database

A simple spreadsheet is enough. One row per ad, with columns like these:

ColumnExample
CompetitorName and page
Ad start dateAs shown in the Ad Library
HookThe opening line or first seconds
OfferFree consultation, discount, lead magnet
FormatImage, video, carousel
AnglePain, result, proof, urgency, price
Call to actionBook a call, WhatsApp, sign up
NotesAnything you noticed, including doubts

Review it on a schedule, for example weekly or monthly. Ads change, and the trend over time is more useful than a single snapshot.

Read the Ad Library with healthy skepticism

  • It shows ads, not results. The Ad Library does not show spend, conversions or profit for ordinary commercial ads. A running ad is not a proven ad.
  • Long-running is a hint, not proof. An ad that has run for months may be performing, or may simply not have been switched off.
  • Coverage varies. What appears depends on Meta's transparency rules and region, so treat it as a partial picture.
  • Don't copy. Study the angle and the offer, then make your own version for your own audience.

Use AI to find the patterns faster

Once your sheet has 50 to 100 ads, an AI assistant can group them and surface repeating themes far faster than reading by eye. Check its output against the ads, because it can over-generalize.

Use it responsibly

  • The Ad Library is a public transparency tool. Using it for research is fine.
  • Do not copy a competitor's creative, copy or brand assets. Take the insight, not the ad.
  • Don't assume their strategy fits your business. Test ideas on a small budget first.

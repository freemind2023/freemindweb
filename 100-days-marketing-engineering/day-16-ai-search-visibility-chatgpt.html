ChatGPT Might Recommend Your Competitor Instead of You

By Aryan Ahire, Founder — Free Mind Consultancy · Published October 18, 2026 · Part of 100 Days of Marketing Engineering

If a prospect asks ChatGPT or Perplexity “best [your category] in [your city]” and it names a competitor instead of you, you lose that lead without ever seeing it in a dashboard — there's no impression, no click, no bounce rate to notice. More buying research starts this way every month, and most businesses have no idea where they currently stand.

Why this is a different game than ranking on Google

A Google results page shows ten blue links ranked by an algorithm you can study and influence with known signals. An AI assistant's answer is generated, not ranked — the model decides what to mention based on what it has learned and, for some platforms, what it retrieves live from the web at the moment you ask. There's no single “position 1” to chase.

How to actually check where you stand

Ask directly→ Vary the phrasing→ Note who's named→ Repeat over time
  1. Ask the question a real customer would ask — not your brand name, the actual need (“best ghostwriter for a business book in India”, not “Free Mind reviews”).
  2. Try it across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini — each pulls from different sources and will give different answers.
  3. Vary the phrasing 3–4 times — one prompt tells you almost nothing; a pattern across several tells you something real.
  4. Track it over time, not as a one-off check — this is exactly what a tool like Vimarsh automates for a business that wants an ongoing number instead of a one-time snapshot.

What actually seems to influence a mention

  • Content that directly answers the question in plain language — a page that opens with a quotable, self-contained definition is easier for a model to lift than one that buries the answer in paragraph four.
  • Being cited elsewhere — reviews, directories, press coverage, and forum answers all feed the sources these models draw from.
  • Consistent entity information — the same business name, description, and details everywhere reduce ambiguity for a model trying to identify who you actually are.

Read this with healthy skepticism

  • There is no guaranteed way to “rank #1” in an AI answer the way there is for a search result.
  • Results vary between models, between sessions, and over time — a single good or bad answer proves little on its own.
  • Treat this as a signal to monitor, not a scoreboard to game.

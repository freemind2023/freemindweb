Your Competitor Changed Their Offer. Did You Notice?

By Aryan Ahire, Founder — Free Mind Consultancy · Published September 26, 2026 · Part of 100 Days of Marketing Engineering

Your competitor might change their offer before you even notice. Most people check a competitor's website once and forget about it. But what if they quietly change the headline, the pricing, the call to action or the offer? Those changes can show you what they are testing in the market.

Why one look is not enough

A competitor's page is not a fixed thing. Teams rewrite headlines, test prices, swap offers and rearrange sections all the time. If you look once, you only see one moment. If you look on a schedule and keep a record, you can see the direction of travel. That record is a timeline of their marketing moves.

What to watch on a landing page

  • Headline and subheadline — the core promise and who it is for.
  • Offer — free trial, free consultation, discount, bonus, guarantee.
  • Pricing — plans, amounts, what is included, whether prices are shown at all.
  • Call to action — the button text and where it sits.
  • Social proof — testimonials, logos, numbers, badges.
  • Urgency — countdowns, limited slots, deadlines.
  • Structure — sections added, removed or reordered.

The workflow

Pick pages→ Snapshot→ Compare→ Log→ Interpret
  • Pick pages — for each competitor choose the homepage, the pricing page and their main landing page.
  • Snapshot — on a fixed schedule, weekly is enough, save the date, the URL, the page text and a screenshot.
  • Compare — put the newest snapshot next to the previous one and list what changed, field by field.
  • Log — add a dated line to your timeline.
  • Interpret — ask what they might be testing, and whether the same direction shows up again.

Build the change timeline

A simple spreadsheet is enough. One row per change:

ColumnExample
DateThe date of the newer snapshot
Competitor and pageName and URL
FieldHeadline, price, CTA, offer, section
BeforeThe exact old text
AfterThe exact new text
HypothesisWhat they may be testing
ConfidenceLow, medium or high

Read the changes with healthy skepticism

  • One change proves nothing. Look for the same direction across several snapshots before calling it a strategy.
  • It might be an A/B test. Different visitors can see different versions, so a change you see may not be the version everyone sees.
  • Location and device matter. Pages can differ by country, device or traffic source.
  • You see the change, not the result. You cannot tell from the page alone whether the new version performs better.
  • Don't react to every tweak. Act on patterns, not on single edits.

Use AI to compare snapshots

Once you have two snapshots, an AI assistant can compare them field by field and draft your timeline entry. Check its output against the source text.

You are a competitive intelligence analyst. Below are two snapshots of the same competitor landing page.

Competitor and URL: [name / URL]
Snapshot A (older) - date: [date]
[paste page text]

Snapshot B (newer) - date: [date]
[paste page text]

1. List every change as: field | before | after. Quote exact text. Say "no change" for fields that did not change.
2. Classify each change: copy test, offer change, pricing change, positioning shift, new proof, CTA change, or cosmetic.
3. For each meaningful change give a hypothesis about what they may be testing, with a confidence level, and mark it as inference.
4. Write one timeline log entry per meaningful change.
5. Do not guess traffic, conversions or results. Use only the text above.

Use it responsibly

  • Monitor public pages only, and respect the site's terms and robots rules.
  • Keep it light. Checking a page weekly is fine; automated tools that request a site heavily are not.
  • Don't copy a competitor's headline, copy or offer. Use the change as a prompt to think about your own.

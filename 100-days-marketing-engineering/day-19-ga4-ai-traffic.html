Day 19 / 100GA4 · AI Traffic

ChatGPT Is Stealing Your Analytics (Sort Of)

By Aryan Ahire, Founder — Free Mind Consultancy · Published October 18, 2026 · Part of 100 Days of Marketing Engineering

A visit that started as “ask ChatGPT about X” and ended on your site shows up somewhere in GA4 — usually misclassified as generic Referral traffic, or worse, as Direct, hiding real AI-driven demand inside a bucket that tells you nothing about where it actually came from.

Where AI referral traffic actually shows up

In GA4, go to Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition, and add a Session source filter for these hostnames:

  • chat.openai.com / chatgpt.com
  • perplexity.ai
  • gemini.google.com
  • copilot.microsoft.com

Why it's undercounted even when you look

This isn't a GA4 bug — it's a measurement gap everyone is currently dealing with. Many AI answer links don't pass clean referrer data, and some opens happen inside an in-app browser or webview that registers as Direct traffic instead of a referral, because the referrer header never makes it through.

A rough fix worth setting up

Open Explorations→ Build a free-form report→ Filter by AI hostnames→ Save as a dashboard tile

Build a custom channel group or a saved Exploration isolating these hostnames, so you have one number to track over time — even an imperfect number is better than none, as long as you treat it as directional.

Read this with healthy skepticism

  • Don't treat this figure as precise — it's a floor, not a ceiling, because of the referrer-stripping issue above.
  • Watch the trend over weeks, not any single day's count.

Free Mind's SEO work runs on real GA4 and Search Console data, including tracking AI-referral traffic as its own signal, not folding it into noise.
